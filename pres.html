01 / An interesting relationship · birth year × estimated belief

Birth period and belief in God

Across the real personas in this dataset, later birth years tend to be associated with lower estimated probability that God exists.

9,000 real personas Spearman ρ = −0.436 Median by birth period

The signal
−0.44

Spearman’s ρ for birth year and estimated belief. A moderate negative monotonic association in these records.

These values are persona estimates, not direct historical survey answers.

OBSERVE

The median generally falls across later birth cohorts.

INTERPRET

Later-born personas are coded with lower estimated religious belief.

LIMIT

The sample is not a representative population. Association does not show cause.

02 / The most surprising finding · expectation versus data

The most surprising finding

I expected the desire to live forever to be common across eras. Instead, this dataset shows a striking difference between the earliest and latest birth cohorts.

Boolean → 1 / 0 Group mean → percent Real personas only

Who is coded as wanting to live forever?

Percent of real personas in each birth cohort · hover for counts

Hover over a bar to explore the data ✦
The unexpected gap
34.5% → 3.2%

BCE versus 1950–1999: a difference of about 31 percentage points in how these personas are coded.

EXPECTATION

Would the wish to live forever be similar across eras?

RESULT

It is much more frequent among the BCE personas.

CAUTION

Selection and persona construction may shape the difference.

Key nuance: 1900–1949 rises slightly compared with 1800–1899. Describe an overall pattern, not an uninterrupted decline.

Birth-cohort differences are not direct evidence of change within the same people over time.

METHOD

Filter real personas, divide birth years into periods, then compute percent True.

RESULT

The earliest cohort is highest; the latest is lowest.

LIMIT

Period sizes vary. These are selected personas rather than a random sample.
